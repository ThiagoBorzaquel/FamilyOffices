Chapter 24

Twenty principles learned over four centuries

"Wealth can be created in one generation. Perpetual wealth must be built over many." A sample of the principles that close the book.

01

Wealth is a consequence, not a goal

Families that pursue only money accumulate assets. Families that pursue purpose build a legacy.

02

Building is different from preserving

Creating wealth requires courage. Preserving it requires discipline — and confusing the two is one of the greatest causes of wealth destruction.

04

Governance outweighs genius

Wealth rarely disappears due to lack of intelligence. It disappears due to lack of organization.

05

Succession begins before inheritance

Transferring wealth is an event. Preparing successors is a process that starts in childhood.

08

Time multiplies everything

Compound interest, knowledge, relationships, and reputation: everything grows when given enough time.

About the author

Thiago Borzacchiello

Independent researcher, investor, and scholar at the intersection of wealth, technology, family governance, and long-term wealth building.

He has dedicated thousands of hours to studying the history of great fortunes, the evolution of financial markets, Family Offices, and estate succession — seeking to understand not only how great families became wealthy, but why some preserved and grew their wealth for centuries while so many others vanished in just a few generations.

For him, wealth does not represent merely financial assets: it represents freedom, responsibility, purpose, and the opportunity to build a legacy capable of benefiting future generations.

True wealth is not what one generation accumulates. It is what many generations manage to honor.
